// lab 07 · imagery · the question of a figure

Not a mascot. A plate.

Behind this headline is Federal Hall and the Washington statue, drawn for Harper's Weekly on 24 May 1884 and dithered to one bit. It carries no colour of its own — it is a native-scale crop painted with --r-voice, at 14%. Read the sentence first and the building second. That order is the whole rule.

Where atmosphere becomes decoration

One plate, one class, five values of --l7-strength. The plate is cropped at native pixel scale in every cell; only opacity changes. Shrinking a 1-bit dither would average its dots into grey and invalidate the comparison. The usable band ends when the engraving competes with type for first read. Spec §4: the void is electrical depth, not emptiness. A figure may give the void a reason to be dark, but may not become the subject.

One asset, both registers

This is the argument for shipping engravings as masks rather than images. The plate has no colour, so it inherits the register's voice ink like any other contract element — amber under Console, peach under Broadcast. A coloured PNG would be a fifth colour that ignores the register law and needs a second file to fix. Both panes below load the same panic-portico-1884.png.

What a mascot would look like here

Same asset, opaque, centred, at mascot scale. It is not ugly — that is the trap. It is a character, and the moment a surface has one, every plate afterwards is either carrying it or conspicuously missing it. It also fails the governing test on contact: a serious operator broadcasting from a live desk does not have a logo creature standing behind them.

the difference between these two sections is one number
Recommendation

Adopt the plate, refuse the mascot. Concretely: one sourced engraving family, delivered as 1-bit masks, tinted by the register, cropped at native pixel scale, capped at 0.26, and restricted to Broadcast heroes, covers and section openers — never behind a Console dashboard, never behind live data, never on a morning brief where a number must be read at 06:00. Spec §5 gains a short imagery clause; the contract gains one class and two tokens only after a second consumer asks for it.

The part worth arguing about is scope creep. One plate is a texture. Six plates is an illustration library, then a commissioning budget, then a house illustrator — which is the imagery version of where design systems quietly become layout frameworks. Cap it at a family from a single source, or do not start.
