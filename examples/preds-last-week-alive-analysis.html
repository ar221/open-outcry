01 / READ THIS FIRST: WHAT THE VOD CAN AND CANNOT PROVE

Read this first: what the VOD can and cannot prove

This report analyzes chronological transcript crops. The primary evidence is Predz's own six-hour stream, creator-provided metadata, and English auto-captions checked in timestamped phases. The transcript is reliable for his spoken reasoning but not every fine-grained map position or speaker attribution. Visual settings shown without narration were not guessed.

This was not Ty's exact world. The tournament used a static Rosewood spawn/seed, seven-day scoring, normal population ramping toward insane, extremely rare loot, immediate water loss, quick power loss, blood-and-saliva transmission, fast mortality, and a slim (<5%) random-sprinter chance. Ty's inspected preset is materially nastier in one critical respect: random speed with 33% sprinters at 75–100% speed, multi-hit off, saliva-only transmission, and 0–12-hour bite mortality. Use Predz as decision-process evidence, not proof that the same pull size is safe in our run.

02 / THE WINNING THESIS

The winning thesis

Predz's run had four phases:

  1. Day 1 — build the engine. Establish a low-density Rosewood-edge house, secure axes and a hacksaw, forage and carve for easy skill points, and sleep in a controlled bed.
  2. Days 2–3 — buy map access. Find a vehicle, siphon fuel, collect early stamps while population is still navigable, and refuse prestige locations such as Louisville and West Point.
  3. Days 4–6 — cash the familiar ground. Return to Rosewood, diversify cheap skill levels and kills, maintain moodles, and stop crossing the map.
  4. Day 7 — preserve the book. Probe horde night from the vehicle, use fences and open sightlines, kill isolated sprinters, then reject the prison because the survival payout is worth more than the risky objective.

The run is best understood as mobility + timing + optionality. Combat skill made the plan executable; it was not the plan itself.

03 / THE BUILD

The build

Exact character

Occupation: Lumberjack

Occupation seed: Axe 2, Maintenance 1, Strength 1, plus Ax-pert.

Positive traits (44 points): Dextrous, Wakeful, Gymnast, Fast Learner, Bushcrafter, Athletic, Strong.

Negative traits (44 points): Illiterate, Thin-skinned, Fear of Blood, Conspicuous, Hearty Appetite, Prone to Illness, Slow Healer, Clumsy, High Thirst, Weak Stomach.

Leftover points: 0.

Starting skill levels: Axe 2; Carving 1; Fitness 9; Foraging 1; Knapping 1; Lightfooted 1; Maintenance 2; Nimble 1; Strength 10. Total: 28 levels, which the live scoring mod credited as 700 starting points.

Why it made sense here

  • Illiterate was a seven-day trade. Predz said he would not spend a tournament day reading. It bought the points needed for immediate physical strength, but knowingly closed the written-riddle lane (00:48:17–00:48:43; 00:51:19–00:51:33).
  • Wakeful converted the fixed week into more operating hours. “We need every minute we can get” (00:48:33–00:48:35).
  • Gymnast seeded Nimble. That gives immediate value in repeated single-target melee (00:48:35–00:48:37).
  • Bushcrafter was the scoring engine. It seeded several skills and let him convert trees, saplings, logs and foraging into weapons, food opportunities, Carving XP and Foraging XP (00:48:38–00:50:53; 04:30:37–04:31:22).
  • Athletic + Strong + Lumberjack made axe combat cheap. Strength 10 supported one-/two-shot kills; Fitness 9 preserved movement and recovery; Ax-pert accelerated the weapon he already knew best (00:56:37–00:57:03; 04:33:38–04:33:54).
  • Fast Learner amplified every planned skill threshold. Tournament points came from breadth, not only Axe.

Why we should not copy it wholesale

Illiterate is a short-horizon scoring choice, not a sound default for a long modded campaign with books, magazines, recipes, maps and technology. Thin-skinned, Clumsy, Conspicuous and Slow Healer become much uglier with one-third sprinters. Hearty Appetite and High Thirst attack exactly the scarce-food/immediate-water problem. Fear of Blood created a recurring wash/stress tax. The transferable core is the physical and skill engine; the negative package needs rebuilding for our actual save.

04 / PHASE BREAKDOWN

Phase breakdown

00 — Before the bell: define what not to do

Predz began by deleting entire scoring lanes from consideration. Riddles and plushies were out because Illiterate made them inaccessible; Louisville and other high-density prestige targets were already suspect; the plan was Bushcraft XP, ordinary kills, and reachable stamps. That matters: he did not start with a giant wish list. He started with a bounded portfolio.

Key evidence: 00:48:17–00:52:23.

01 — Day 1: Rosewood edge, not Rosewood centre

He spawned in southeast Rosewood and built a hub near a birch mixed forest. The house gave him a bed, storage and a known return point. The forest supplied forage food and raw material for Carving. Early combat was purposeful: check the group for sprinters, let the shamblers approach, loop them into manageable frontage, then use Axeman speed and animation cancelling.

Loot priority was narrow: rags, hacksaw, axe, hammer, a screwdriver if found, basic medicine, food for the outing. He rejected clutter, duplicate axes, a crowbar he did not need, baking equipment and long-horizon comfort. He stored excess gear, constructed a campfire after the immediate outage, processed logs into score-bearing Carving work, extinguished the fire, and slept in the house rather than improvising a dangerous night.

What mattered: a known bed and return route were more valuable than a glamorous landmark.

Key evidence: 00:51:53–01:20:41.

02 — Day 2: mobility is the gate

The declared requirement was simple: “We got to get a car today” (01:22:38). Predz pushed north, took the 100-point courthouse stamp, searched vehicles, and avoided fully clearing the police station because its likely loot did not advance the route. He used a fence as a fighting surface, counted the remaining zombies, checked behind himself, and moved on when the required space was open.

A Dashbull at the fire-department lot became the run's mobility platform. It had little fuel, so he found a hose, siphoned nearby vehicles, and cleared threats before standing still to transfer fuel. The truck eventually reached a full tank. This was the real unlock: not a weapon upgrade, but a machine that converted distant stamps into short exposures.

He then returned to his southeast base rather than sleeping in the car. That cost tempo, but protected recovery. He also rejected Speed Demon because uncontrolled speed could end the run.

What mattered: the vehicle was an extraction system first. Fuel, restart reliability and departure geometry governed the route.

Key evidence: 01:20:41–01:53:29.

03 — Days 3–4: the early stamp circuit

Predz's route logic was explicit: March Ridge, Muldraugh, Fallas Lake, Country Club, Riverside; skip West Point and Louisville. He preferred several reliable 100-point stops over a theoretical 300-point death sentence. “We're just going to take the easy ones” (02:05:53–02:06:31).

His first important stop-loss came in Muldraugh. He considered the police/jail armory, but darkness, unfamiliar Build 42 layout and no flashlight turned the raid into dead time. Chat persuaded him to retry; after more wasted time he finally aborted. This is a recurring pattern: his initial judgment was usually right, but competition and chat could talk him across his own threshold.

Then the run nearly broke. Distracted while driving, he crashed and injured his arm. From that point, ordinary stand-up fighting stopped being acceptable. At Fallas Lake he left the engine running, ran in, grabbed the stamp and left. At the Country Club he proceeded despite the bad arm, a sprinter, darkness and no flashlight. He used fences to restrict approach direction, swapped boots for sneakers—“Sneakers keep us alive”—and ultimately slept on an exposed bench after overextending.

The clever part was adaptation: when strength failed, he changed the interaction model from clear-and-loot to dash-and-extract. The bad part was crossing the threshold in the first place.

What mattered: moodles and injuries changed the route, not merely combat damage.

Key evidence: 01:53:29–02:54:52.

04 — Day 4–5: stop crossing the map

The truck began stalling. A West Point run was no longer a stamp decision; it was a vehicle-reliability gamble, so he rejected it. He searched for a replacement, found no viable swap, and pivoted from map objectives to nearby skill thresholds, weapons and recovery.

At a rural farm he managed drowsiness, agitation, food, a foot scratch and a compromised sleeping building. A basement zombie was generating stress and waking him; he left rather than pretending the building was safe because the doors were closed.

Returning through Muldraugh, he exploited an alarm that had pulled zombies north and approached from the less pressured side. He looted a knife, revolver and ammunition, but refused the lower floor because his foot and uncertainty made the marginal loot poor value. Back near Rosewood he rested between groups, rotated weapons for cheap level-one points, and withdrew from a police station once tired—even with only a few zombies apparently remaining.

What mattered: he no longer needed another expedition. Familiar ground could pay through skills, kills and survival.

Key evidence: 02:55:03–03:50:28.

05 — Day 5–6: density shaping, not city clearing

Predz used the rural edge during the helicopter event rather than allowing it to drag pressure through his principal Rosewood workspace. Afterward he re-entered town slowly to read the new population.

The near-death came during a window pull. The group became too large, a zombie lunged through, and he was scratched. His immediate reaction was “That's game.” Once he confirmed no bite and no rapid infection signs, he repriced the run: the extra ammunition inside the police station was no longer necessary. He moved to Spiffo's, where fighting could also prepare the burn objective, kept the van beside him, checked for sprinters, and opened both exits before continuing.

The window incident is crucial because it shows both sides of geometry. Windows can serialize a group, but if the pull exceeds your timing window, the landing/lunge animation collapses the choke into a contact injury. Predz survived; the tactic did not become safe retroactively.

What mattered: redirect density, approach from edges, peel only what blocks the target, recover, and keep the vehicle exit open.

Key evidence: 03:50:28–04:20:12.

06 — Day 7: horde night as changing risk states

Predz did not simply stand and defeat the final horde. He moved through a sequence:

  1. Observe from inside the van until the speed mix is visible.
  2. Stay near fences to disrupt pursuit and expose fast contacts.
  3. Pull and eliminate sprinters separately.
  4. Use the shotgun while it produces control plus Aiming/Reloading progression.
  5. Switch to the axe when shells become scarce.
  6. Reposition with the van and drag pressure away.
  7. Park tight against a building, lock the doors and use the vehicle-wall combination to reduce approach angles.

He monitored fuel, ammunition, weapon durability and visibility. He disliked trees because they hid lateral approaches. He accepted hood damage only because it was the last day, then shut off the engine to conserve fuel—an action he himself called “very ballsy.” The organizer later praised the fence + vehicle position because it preserved an escape if the horde became unmanageable.

This is not “fight the horde.” It is preserve two exits at every stage.

Key evidence: 04:53:47–05:14:11.

07 — The winning decision: do not enter the prison

With roughly 45 minutes remaining, the Rosewood Prison offered about 200 risky points. Survival was worth 300 in Predz's calculation—and later clarified as 400. The prison required enclosed entry, rear exposure to sprinters and a vehicle whose failure could trap him. He declined it.

He finished the week at 3,426 points.

This was the purest expression of the whole run: the relevant comparison was not “200 points versus nothing.” It was 200 uncertain points versus a larger guaranteed payout plus completion.

Key evidence: 05:17:42–05:20:25.

05 / HIGH-POPULATION NAVIGATION DOCTRINE

High-population navigation doctrine

1. Scan for the fastest contact, not the average crowd

A mixed horde is defined by the unseen sprinter. Predz repeatedly checked groups specifically for fast zombies, let sprinters separate from shamblers, and removed them before sustained melee. In Ty's 33% configuration, assume every substantial pull contains several.

2. Fight for access, not extermination

Clear the courthouse approach, driver door, fuel-transfer space or exit lane. Do not clear the whole police station merely because you began. The target is the interaction and safe extraction, not ownership of the block.

3. Use known open ground

Open ground reveals lateral approaches and gives the vehicle space to accelerate. Trees hide sprinters. Interiors multiply unknown doors and corners. Predz's stated all-sprinter fallback was a field and a van.

4. Use fences as time, not safety

Fences can trip zombies, generate audible warnings and reduce frontage. They also create crawlers, lunges and traps. Predz was briefly fenced in during the finale. Use a fence only when the landing side is visible and the second exit is memorized.

5. Anchor dangerous work to an exit

At stamps and Spiffo's, the vehicle stayed close. Doors were opened/unlocked before the next pull. Fueling happened after nearby threats were killed. Every stationary interaction must be priced as a moment when the character cannot immediately fight or flee.

6. Reset between groups

Predz stopped to recover before re-engaging. Drowsiness, slow swing speed, a bad arm, lack of stamina regeneration or an unreliable vehicle were route constraints. If the character cannot kill one sprinter cleanly right now, the plan must become noncombat extraction.

7. Treat attention as a survival resource

He nearly walked into a stealth zombie while reading chat, misread 10 p.m. as 10 a.m. at the Country Club, forgot to bandage a wound, and nearly ended the run while eating and driving. Menus, maps, chat and inventory belong in hard-safe positions.

07 / TOURNAMENT LOGIC VERSUS NORMAL SURVIVAL

Tournament logic versus normal survival

Transfer directly

  • Familiar hub plus short, purpose-built routes.
  • Vehicle as extraction infrastructure.
  • Identify and isolate sprinters before engaging walkers.
  • Reset stamina between groups.
  • Reject interiors with unknown layout and optional reward.
  • Use open terrain for visibility and fences only with an exit.
  • Let injuries and drowsiness cancel objectives.
  • Carry a fallback weapon and keep escape geometry simple.
  • Compare every objective with what is already banked.

Translate, do not copy

  • “Points per risk” becomes capability gained per risk: water, food, medicine, transport, knowledge or redundancy.
  • Stamp circuits become early trips for fixed essentials while roads remain navigable.
  • Skill diversification becomes controlled training near a safe fallback, not live experimentation during migration.
  • The vehicle-wall position becomes an emergency shelter, not a permanent answer.

Leave in the tournament

  • Illiterate to save reading time.
  • Burning Spiffo's for 25 points.
  • Hunting marginal skill levels during horde night.
  • Accepting hood damage because the clock is almost finished.
  • Sleeping on an exposed bench because a one-week score window is closing.
  • Continuing after “this is a bad idea” because a leaderboard rewards the upside.
08 / FOR TY'S COLLECTION: THE HARSHER TRANSLATION

For Ty's Collection: the harsher translation

Predz had a slim sprinter chance. We have a configured 33%. That changes the doctrine:

  • A pull large enough to contain one tournament sprinter may contain several for us.
  • The car cannot be parked merely nearby; driver access and the departure lane must stay uncontested.
  • Fence use needs a visible landing side and a second route before the first swing.
  • Dense trees and blind building corners should be treated as active threats, not neutral terrain.
  • Thin-skinned/Clumsy/Slow Healer are far less defensible.
  • A route that depends on fighting after an arm or leg injury is already failed.
  • “Drive in, grab, leave” remains useful only when the interaction is known and the engine is reliable.
  • Our retreat threshold must fire earlier than Predz's. His win demonstrates excellent recovery from bad positions; it does not validate entering them.

Strongest new retreat rule

Once you have already said “this is a bad idea,” chat, sunk cost and one more container do not count as new information. Leave.

Predz often identified the correct threshold, crossed it, and survived through skill or luck. Winning the tournament does not make each local gamble correct.

10 / POST-RUN NOTES

Post-run notes

Predz described the event as an absolute blast and enjoyed returning to Zomboid. He split the $250 first-place prize between his two clippers/moderators, Emma and Foo; this was community compensation, not a charity donation. He explicitly named the Country Club time-reading lapse—jumping the fence at 10 p.m. while thinking it was 10 a.m.—and acknowledged that it consumed the lighter needed for the planned Spiffo's burn. He also regretted missing the Muldraugh beaver while tunnel-visioned on the armory.

The field's comparison supports the tactical reading. Casters said the common meta became “get established, get a car, collect stamps.” Claire performed the stamp route later, after the population rose; Scrubbly's high-pressure approach might have overtaken Predz without the laceration. Predz's differentiator was combining early stamps with broad skill grinding and then actually de-risking the finish.
